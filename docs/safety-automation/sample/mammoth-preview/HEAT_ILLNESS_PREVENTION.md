This plan implements 8 CCR **3395** (outdoor) and 8 CCR **3396** (indoor, when indoor temperature reaches 82°F). It is written for DOCON construction and interiors work in California. It replaces all prior heat manuals, including any copy that referenced another employer.

Project-specific directions, hospital, and who calls 911 are in the **Project Safety Packet**. This company plan stays the same on every job.

## 2. Temperature triggers (air temperature, not Heat Index)

Heat Index may be used as extra information. Shade and high-heat **triggers are thermometer / reliable weather temperature**.

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Any outdoor work | Water, training, emergency procedures, this written plan, shade on request |
| Outdoor **above 80°F** | Shade must be **present** and close to the work |
| Outdoor **95°F** | High-heat procedures (construction is a listed industry) |
| Indoor **82°F** | Indoor water, cool-down area below 82°F, acclimatization watch, this plan |
| Indoor **87°F** (or 82°F with heat-trapping clothing or high radiant heat) | Engineering controls first, then administrative, then PPE |

## 3. Water

- Fresh, pure, suitably cool, free.
- As close as practicable to the work.
- At least **1 quart per employee per hour** for the shift. At least 2 quarts per person at the start of shift if it will be replenished.
- Encourage small amounts often, up to 4 cups per hour when people are sweating.
- Superintendent checks containers every 30–60 minutes when it is hot; ices water when needed; moves water with the crew.
- Individual bottles are used when terrain or a floor plate makes a cooler too far away.

## 4. Shade (outdoor)

When temperature **exceeds 80°F**, shade is present the entire time employees are there.

Shade is enough for **every employee on a recovery or rest period at the same time**, sitting in a normal posture, fully in the shade, not touching each other. Meal-period shade fits everyone who stays on site.

Shade is as close as practicable. Canopies, umbrellas, building shadow, or an **air-conditioned vehicle or trailer with the A/C on** count. A closed truck in the sun does not.

Below 80°F, provide the same shade or provide it promptly when someone asks.

**Preventative cool-down rest:** Anyone may sit in the shade when they feel they need it. Monitor them and ask about symptoms. They stay in the shade. They are not ordered back until symptoms are gone, and not in less than **5 minutes** plus the time to reach shade. If they show signs of heat illness, provide first aid and emergency response.

## 5. High-heat procedures (outdoor 95°F) — construction

DOCON is construction / interiors. These apply at 95°F:

1. **Communication** that actually works on that site (voice, radio, or phone with confirmed reception). If cell service is not reliable, the Project Safety Packet must list the radio or runner method.
2. **Observation method:** On crews of 20 or fewer, the superintendent or designee watches the crew. If someone works alone, the superintendent checks in on a set schedule. A buddy system may be used in addition.
3. **Designated 911 caller:** the person named on the Project Safety Packet. Everyone on the crew is told that name at the pre-shift meeting.
4. Remind the crew to drink water throughout the shift.
5. **Pre-shift meeting** covering high-heat procedures, water, and the right to a cool-down rest.
6. **New employees:** close observation for the first 14 days of outdoor work, unless they state at hire that they have done similar outdoor work at least 10 of the last 30 days for 4+ hours a day.

The agriculture-only rule of a mandatory 10-minute rest every 2 hours at 95°F does **not** apply to DOCON unless we are performing agricultural work. Extra rest is still encouraged.

## 6. Acclimatization

Watch everyone during a heat wave (predicted high at least 80°F and at least 10°F hotter than the average high of the prior five days).

Ease new or returning employees into heavier work over the first days. Assign a buddy when practical.

## 7. Indoor heat (8 CCR 3396)

When employees work indoors and the space reaches **82°F** (unconditioned building, conex, shop, job trailer without A/C):

- Same water rule.
- At least one **cool-down area kept below 82°F**, shielded from sun and radiant heat, large enough for employees on rest, as close as practicable.
- Measure and record temperature or heat index in that space when it is expected to hit the trigger. The superintendent notes it on the daily inspection / PTP.
- At 87°F (or 82°F with heat-trapping PPE or high radiant heat), use feasible controls first: ventilation, fans that do not blow only hot air, schedule heavy work earlier, rest cycle, then cooling PPE if needed.

## 8. Emergencies

Project Safety Packet holds: exact address, 911 directions, muster, hospital name/phone, who calls 911, cell-coverage note.

Employee: report symptoms in yourself or a coworker at once. Move the person to shade or a cool area. Call 911 if the superintendent is not immediately available or the person does not improve.

Supervisor: confirm communication works before the shift. Know the directions. Provide first aid. Cool the person while waiting for EMS. Do not give fluids to an unconscious person.

If the case is a serious illness under 8 CCR 342, Charles Dossett or the packet’s Cal/OSHA caller reports it within 8 hours.

## 9. Training

Before outdoor or covered indoor heat work, employees and supervisors are trained on:

- Personal and environmental risk factors, including clothing and PPE
- This plan’s water, shade, cool-down, high-heat, indoor, and emergency steps
- Acclimatization
- Types and signs of heat illness
- Duty to report symptoms immediately
- How EMS is called and how to give directions to this job
- Supervisors: weather check, heat-wave response, and their duties under this plan

Training is recorded by name, date, and trainer. Dead third-party slide links are not an acceptable program.
