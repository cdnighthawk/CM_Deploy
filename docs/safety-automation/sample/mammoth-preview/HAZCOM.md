# HAZARD COMMUNICATION PROGRAM

**DOCON, INC. dba US Interior Specialties**  
**Administrator:** Charles Dossett  
**Effective:** 2026-01-01  
**Version:** 0.1.0-draft

This program implements 8 CCR 5194 and 29 CFR 1910.1200 as adopted in California. It applies whenever DOCON employees may be exposed to hazardous chemicals, including adhesives, solvents, paints, fuels, concrete products, drywall compounds, and cleaners.

## 1. Responsible person

Charles Dossett maintains this program, the master chemical list, and SDS access.  
The project superintendent maintains the **job chemical list** in the Project Safety Packet and makes SDS available on that site.

## 2. Chemical inventory

- Company master list lives in the safety portal.
- Each project list is generated from chemicals entered when the project is created or when a product is added.
- No product is used until an SDS is on file and the container is labeled.

## 3. Labels

Factory labels stay on incoming containers. If material is transferred to a secondary container that is not for immediate use by the person who transferred it, the secondary container is labeled with product identity and hazard warning (words, pictures, or symbols).

## 4. Safety Data Sheets

SDS (not “MSDS”) are available to employees during the shift:

- Portal SDS library, and/or
- Binder on the job, and/or
- Manufacturer link recorded on the project chemical list

Employees, their representative, or their physician may request an SDS. Provide it the same shift if it is on file; otherwise obtain it promptly.

## 5. Training

Before working with a hazardous chemical, employees are trained on:

- This program and where to find the list and SDS
- Hazards of the products they use
- How to detect a release (odor, appearance, alarm)
- Protective measures, PPE, and cleanup
- Labels and SDS format

Retrain when a new hazard is introduced.

## 6. Non-routine tasks and unlabeled pipes

The superintendent covers non-routine tasks in the PTP. DOCON does not work on unlabeled pipes until the contents are identified or the controlling employer provides the hazard information.

## 7. Multi-employer sites

When other employers’ workers are exposed to our chemicals, we provide SDS and the identity of the products on request and keep labels intact.

When our workers may be exposed to another employer’s chemicals, the superintendent asks the GC / controlling employer for SDS and precautions before work in that area.

## 8. Recordkeeping

Keep the written program, inventories, SDS for chemicals still in use, and training records. Retain SDS for chemicals no longer in use per 8 CCR 3204 when they serve as exposure records.
