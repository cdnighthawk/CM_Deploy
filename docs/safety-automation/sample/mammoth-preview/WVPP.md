## 1. Policy

DOCON does not tolerate workplace violence. Workplace violence includes:

- The threat or use of physical force against an employee that results in, or has a high likelihood of resulting in, injury, psychological trauma, or stress
- An incident involving the threat or use of a firearm or other dangerous weapon
- Four types under the statute: (1) by a stranger / public; (2) by a customer, client, or visitor; (3) by a current or former employee; (4) personal relationship / domestic violence that follows an employee to work

Employees must report threats and incidents. Reporting will not be punished.

On a GC-controlled site, DOCON follows this plan for its own employees and also follows the controlling employer’s site security rules when those rules are stricter. Threats involving our employees are reported to the GC superintendent the same day. This plan is available to the GC on request.

## 5. How to report

Report immediately to the superintendent or to Charles Dossett at (209) 857-8556.

If there is an immediate danger, **call 911 first**.

Anonymous reports may be made through the portal hazard form (leave name blank) or by an unsigned note to the office. Emergency situations cannot be handled anonymously — call 911.

## 6. Hazard identification

At least when this plan is adopted, when a new type of workplace is added, after a violent incident, and during the annual review, DOCON evaluates:

- Working alone or in isolated rooms
- Public or visitor access (civic, retail, occupied buildings)
- Handling cash or valuable material
- Poor lighting, blocked exits, or no cell service
- Prior incidents or threats on that site
- Domestic-violence concerns an employee chooses to share
- High-conflict meetings or termination meetings

Project-level notes (public interface, occupied building, cell coverage) are pulled from the Project Safety Packet.

## 7. Hazard correction

Examples of corrections:

- Do not work alone in an isolated area when a threat is known
- Keep a charged phone; if cell coverage is unreliable, use the radio or a check-in time listed on the site card
- Use well-lit access and a known muster point
- Ask the GC to control public access to the work area
- Support an employee who has a restraining order that names the workplace
- Remove a person who threatens employees from the job

## 8. Responding to an incident

1. Get to a safe place. Call 911 if anyone is in danger or a weapon is involved.
2. First aid. Do not move a seriously injured person unless they are in immediate danger.
3. Notify Charles Dossett and the GC.
4. Preserve the scene if police request it.
5. Do not discuss facts with the media. Refer media to Charles Dossett.
6. Investigate: what happened, type 1–4, witnesses, conditions, what will change.
7. Record the incident on the **Violent Incident Log** (even if no one was hurt).
8. Offer employees information about the Employee Assistance / workers’ compensation medical path as applicable.
9. Review this plan and retrain if the incident shows a gap.

## 9. Emergency procedures

Project Safety Packet lists muster point, who calls 911, hospital, and 911 directions.

If an active threat is in the building: evade if you can; hide if you cannot leave; defend only as a last resort. When police arrive, empty hands visible, follow commands.

## 10. Training

All employees receive this plan:

- When first hired
- When assigned to a new site with different violence hazards (for example, occupied public building)
- At least annually
- After a violent incident, for employees in that area

Training covers: this plan; how to report; how to recognize escalating behavior; how to respond to a threat or incident; the violent incident log (that it exists, not other people’s names).

Supervisors are also trained on investigation and non-retaliation.

## 11. Violent Incident Log

The administrator keeps a log of every workplace violence incident, including threats. The log includes date, time, location, type 1–4, a description, whether a weapon was involved, number of people involved, and corrective action. Names of employees who experienced the incident are kept only as needed and are not posted.

Retention: **5 years**.
