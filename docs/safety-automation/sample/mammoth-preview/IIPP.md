## 1. Purpose and scope

This IIPP describes how DOCON identifies hazards, corrects them, trains employees, investigates incidents, and gives employees access to this written program. It applies to office work and all field operations in California, including work as a subcontractor on another employer’s site.

Project details (address, muster point, hospital, competent persons, tools in use) live in the **Project Safety Packet** generated for each job. This IIPP does not change from job to job.

## 2. Responsibility

Charles Dossett is the IIPP Administrator and has authority to implement this program.

The project superintendent named in the Project Safety Packet is responsible for daily implementation on that job: inspections, pre-task plans, toolbox talks, water and shade, and stop-work.

Every employee may stop work that presents an imminent hazard and must report hazards without fear of reprisal.

## 3. Employee compliance

DOCON ensures safe work by:

1. Giving every employee this IIPP and the current Project Safety Packet for their job.
2. Training before work begins and when tasks or hazards change.
3. Recognizing people who report hazards and follow the rules.
4. Retraining after an unsafe act.
5. Discipline, up to removal from the project or employment, for willful or repeated violations.

Rules are enforced the same way for DOCON employees and, to the extent we control the work, anyone we supervise.

## 4. Communication

Safety information is provided in English and English and Spanish.

Methods:

- New-hire orientation covering this IIPP, how to report, heat, violence prevention, hazard communication, and PPE.
- Toolbox / accident-prevention talks at least every **10 working days** on construction jobs (8 CCR 1509).
- Daily pre-task plan (PTP) before work.
- Posting at the job and electronic access on the company portal.
- Direct report to the superintendent or to Charles Dossett at (209) 857-8556.

**Anonymous reporting:** An employee may report a hazard without giving a name by calling (209) 857-8556, using the portal “Report a hazard” form and leaving the name blank, or handing a note to the office. DOCON will not retaliate for a good-faith safety report.

## 5. Hazard identification and inspections

Hazards are identified by:

- Reviewing the scope flags on the Project Safety Packet before mobilization.
- A documented inspection when the job starts, when new equipment or chemicals arrive, when work changes, after an injury or near miss, and whenever a new or unrecognized hazard is reported.
- **Daily** visual review by the superintendent or foreman on active construction / interiors sites.
- Pre-task planning with the crew.

Inspection records list the date, who inspected, what was found, and what was corrected.

## 6. Hazard correction

Correct hazards based on severity.

- **Imminent danger:** Remove employees from the area. Only trained people with the right PPE stay to control the hazard. Stop work is authorized.
- **Serious:** Correct the same shift if possible; if not, barricade and schedule before the next exposure.
- **Other:** Correct on a dated plan.

Document the condition, action, person responsible, and close date on the Hazard Abatement Record.

On multi-employer sites, report hazards we do not control to the controlling employer / GC the same day and keep employees out of the danger area until it is fixed or we are released.

## 7. Incident investigation

Every injury, illness, exposure, and near miss is reported to the superintendent the same shift.

The superintendent (or Administrator) will:

1. Make sure the person is treated. Care first, paperwork second.
2. Preserve the scene if it is safe to do so.
3. Interview the employee and witnesses.
4. Identify cause and corrective action.
5. Complete the Incident Investigation Record.

**Cal/OSHA §342:** Report a serious injury, illness, or death to the nearest Cal/OSHA district office as soon as possible but no later than 8 hours (8 CCR 342).  
Charles Dossett or the designated caller on the Project Safety Packet makes that call. Do not wait for a corporate review.

Recordable cases are entered on the Cal/OSHA 300 / 301 system within 7 calendar days when we are required to keep those logs.

## 8. Training

Training is given:

- When this IIPP is first adopted or substantially revised.
- To each new employee before or at first assignment.
- When an employee is given a new task that has not been trained.
- When new substances, processes, equipment, or previously unrecognized hazards appear.
- To supervisors on the hazards their crews face.

Minimum topics for all employees: this IIPP and how to get it; how to report; emergency and first-aid locations; Code of Safe Practices; PPE; hazard communication; heat illness; workplace violence prevention; job-specific hazards listed on the Project Safety Packet.

Construction toolbox talks at least every 10 working days document ongoing instruction.

Training records include employee name, date, subject, and trainer. Keep IIPP training and inspection records at least one year.

## 9. Employee access to this IIPP

Employees and their designated representatives may have this IIPP by:

1. **Portal:** Unobstructed access to the current version on the company website / app they already use for work, with print and email; or
2. **Copy:** A printed copy no later than **five business days** after a written request. One free printed copy per year; additional copies may be charged reasonable copy cost if the IIPP has not changed.

Implementation records (inspections, rosters) are not required to be attached to the copy given to the employee.
